## The Problem

A while back I was going through a hard stretch and started therapy. One thing I noticed pretty quickly was that by the time I sat down with my therapist, I wasn't really in the same headspace I'd been in when things were difficult. The feelings were harder to remember, harder to access, harder to describe. I figured if I wanted therapy to be as valuable as possible, I'd have to put in the legwork outside of the seesions. I decided if I could capture things at the time of them happening, I'd have something more useful to work with.
 
So I tried journaling. I used pen and paper first, which I'd attempted before and never stuck with. Like with many people, I had a few journals that I'd started and only got a few pages in. While I think there's something special about pen and paper, I've found that the friction is just high enough - having the right tools at the right moment, taking the time to get thoughts out slowly -- that it eventually stops happening. This time, I wrote once and that was about it.
 
I looked at a few digital journals after that. Some felt too sleek, too much like a product someone built to make money rather than something actually useful. One had the calendar visualization I was looking for but came with so many configuration options that setting it up felt like another thing to manage. Another felt more like a productivity tool than a journal. None of them felt right, and I also kept running into the same issue of not wanting to be limited to my phone.
 
I enjoy building things, and at this point, I had a pretty clear picture of what I actually wanted, so I built it myself.

## The Outcome
 
That was maybe the best decision I could have made, not just because I got the tool I wanted, but because it has actually worked. I use it pretty much every day. It's helped me build habits I've tried and failed to build before. It's helped me do things I kept putting off. It's helped me notice patterns in how I feel that I wouldn't have seen otherwise. Most of all, it gives me a quiet, private space where I can process thoughts and feelings and never look at again.

Let me elaborate on that last one a bit. There are a thousand journal options out there, and even more writing applications that would work just as well as a journal. In fact, one year, a Google Doc was my journal for a little while. When I used it, I would write what I needed to write and then highlight it in black so I didn't have to look back at it. That's an important thing for me. I have tendency to cringe about my past self. I have a fear when I write that I or someone else might look back at something I wrote and think things about me I didn't want them or myself to think. Something happens when you put a feeling into words that doesn't happen when you just carry it around in your head, and that fear often prevented me from getting that out of the journals I'd kept. Contour Journal is not a tool for looking back and analyzing your thoughts or tracking your ideas. It's a place to get them out of your head so you can move on. If there's something special or insightful you'd like to revisit later, you can bookmark an entry, but the journal itself is meant to welcome you to brain dump, while maybe keeping the best parts in the form of actions, thankfuls, and the occasional bookmarked entry.

## Check it Out!
 
I realize most people don't have the option of just building the thing they want. That's why I decided to make it available. It's still a small project maintained by one person, but if it's useful to me, maybe it's useful to someone else too.
 
If you want to know more about what it does and why it works the way it does, the [about page](/about) covers that pretty well. Or just [create an account](/auth/register) and see for yourself. The more you put in, the more you get out. And trust me when I say your data is safe. It's encrypted in the database so I won't accidentally read it, there are no 3rd party cookies tracking you (the only cookie is so the site remembers you and you don't have to log in every time), and there are no ads noisily competing for your attention.
